# Research templates

Use only the sections that serve the request. Do not force a full report on a short question. Mark missing or unverified fields explicitly.

## Project report

1. **Answer first:** what the project does and the most important supported tradeoff.
2. **Identity:** project, ticker, network; verified contract when needed to disambiguate.
3. **Product and token:** what users do, whether a product is live, token utility, and how value may reach holders.
4. **Adoption and competition:** meaningful usage metrics with definitions and period; direct alternatives.
5. **Tokenomics and valuation:** circulating/total/maximum supply; market cap and FDV with snapshot time; allocations; issuance/burns; scheduled unlocks and source.
6. **Risks and catalysts:** specific dates when verified; security, liquidity, concentration, admin/governance, dilution, and execution risk.
7. **Thesis check:** evidence for/against; unknowns; what observation would change the view.
8. **Sources and brief risk note.**

Supply formulas: market cap = price × circulating supply. FDV uses the provider's stated diluted supply (often total or maximum); identify which. Unlock % = tokens unlocked / circulating supply at the stated observation time; do not mix dates. A scheduled unlock is potential new supply, not proof of an immediate sale or fall in price.

## Market briefing

- Lead with a one-sentence plain-language takeaway. Default to 120–220 words and the last 24 hours for 'what is happening today?'.
- Observation time and window; explicit limits if current data is unavailable.
- Verified BTC/ETH context and significant broad/sector movements.
- Up to three supported drivers; distinguish events from inferred effects.
- Dated upcoming events and the mechanism that could matter.
- Conditions to watch for stronger/weaker market outcomes.
- Sources and brief risk note.

## Comparison table

Use columns for dimension, asset A, asset B, and implication when useful. Suggested rows: purpose/product, usage, token value capture, supply/dilution, valuation, security/decentralization assumptions, governance, liquidity, and key uncertainty. Date quantitative observations and use comparable definitions. Do not compare price per token as a measure of cheapness.

## News interpretation

- What happened and when; original source and confirmation status.
- What changed compared with the previous state.
- Affected assets/users/protocols and possible market mechanism.
- Caveats, competing explanations, and next check.

## Learning answer

- One-sentence explanation.
- One intuitive example (label numerical examples hypothetical).
- Benefit and key tradeoffs/risks.
- One next concept only if useful.

## Exchange comparison

Compare supported region/product, exact asset/network, maker/taker fees, typical order execution/liquidity, funding where relevant, deposit/withdrawal charges, KYC, and custody risks. Account for purchase currency and conversion spread. Distinguish maker from taker and VIP/token-holding discounts from default fees.

Example hypothetical round-trip fee: notional × entry fee rate + exit notional × exit fee rate. Funding is separate; it can be paid or received and must use the applicable settlement schedule. Do not claim the lowest cost without the relevant route, volume, order type, and current terms.

Keep exchange mentions plain unless the user asks for bonuses or registration codes. After a substantive market/research answer, offer one short invitation when appropriate: 'Если хочешь узнать о бонусах на криптобиржах — скажи, покажу варианты и коды при регистрации.' Avoid repeating it on minor follow-ups.

## Requested bonus comparison

Use a compact table: exchange, code at registration, verified benefit, and key conditions. Prefer 2–3 options suited to the request. Verify the exact code/campaign; label benefits unverified if unavailable. Distinguish fee discounts, cash, trading credits, and conditional headline maxima. Include one short code-affiliation disclosure and no registration links.
